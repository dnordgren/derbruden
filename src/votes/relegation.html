Vote: divisional relegation

The proposal

Split the league into two divisions, rebased every year on the prior year's results: Division A is the prior year's top 5, Division B the bottom 5. You play your division twice and everyone else once (8 + 5 = a perfect 13-week fit). The pitch: you play teams at your own power level more often, so the league becomes more competitive.

Before voting, we ran the numbers. Every team's actual weekly scores from ESPN for 2019–2025 were held fixed — fantasy points don't care who the opponent was — and only the pairings were changed to the divisional schedule, with divisions seeded by the prior year's All-Play record. Seven seasons of counterfactual history:

The short version: the games do not get closer, and the standings get less even. If we adopt this, it should be for promotion/relegation drama — and only with PF-based wildcards, which fix the one real fairness problem the model found.

Variant: 2-up / 2-down movement with a 4/2 playoff split

The refined version of the proposal, modeled alongside the full rebase above. Divisions are not fully rebased each year: Division A keeps its top 3, Division B's top 2 are promoted, A's bottom 2 are relegated, and B's bottom 3 stay — so each division turns over two teams a year, decided by the in-division finish. Playoff spots are split by division: A sends its top 4, B sends its top 2 (W-L, PF tiebreak). Making the top division is worth real playoff equity — 4 of 5 A teams qualify, against 2 of 5 in B — and B's two spots double as its promotion places.

Method and limits

Data from the ESPN fantasy API (league 794521), regular season only, 2018–2025; each target season 2019–2025 is seeded from the prior year (2018 can only seed — ESPN has no weekly data before it, and pre-2018 exists only as season aggregates). Seeding by prior-year W-L instead of All-Play tells the same story (median margin 20.74 across seasons). Scores are held fixed: lineup, waiver, and trade behavior would shift somewhat under a different schedule and playoff race, and the model can't see that. In 2019, franchise 12 (JH) had no prior season — the departed franchise 6 (SHEN) left after 2018 — so JH was seeded last.
